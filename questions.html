The rules behind this map

Numbers from different papers can only be compared if everyone counts the same thing. These are the ten definition rules we use. We checked each one against the published literature; two are still open, and we would value your view on those.

How to read this: compiled with AI assistance from a partial reading of the papers listed (the relevant sections, not whole papers), and not reviewed by a group of specialists. One materials scientist has answered these questions; where the literature is silent, that is one person's view. No agreed MXene reporting standard turned up in our search. «Not found» means our search did not find it, not that it does not exist.

Still open — your view would help

A. A free-standing film tested with metal strips as current collectors

The one specialist we asked filed this under «film on a current collector»; papers often call the same electrode «free-standing». We found no guideline that decides it. We propose recording two things separately: the electrode form, and the current collector used in the cell.

B. Work function measured on Si or SiO₂/Si, next to free-standing films

We found no study that compares MXene work function across substrates. Reported values differ more clearly by method (KPFM against UPS) and by surface chemistry. We propose analysing them together while always recording the substrate, the method, the reference and the measurement environment.

Rules in use

1. Surface-modified MXene in the map

Covalently grafted films still count as that MXene, but need a «surface-modified» label: conductivity falls as grafting increases.

10.1002/adma.202502440 · 10.1038/s41467-018-08169-8

2. Impurities the authors report in the map

If authors say impurities remained and lowered a value, it is not used as a pure reference; the value stays, labelled. We found no published threshold for how much is too much.

10.3390/ma12030353 · 10.1021/acs.chemmater.7b02847

3. MAX-phase quality rule set · no field yet

When a paper reports it, the starting ratio, precursor source and defect level belong in the recipe. Phase purity alone is not enough.

10.1021/acsnano.0c08357 · 10.1016/j.pmatsci.2020.100757

4. Drying and centrifugation rule set · not recorded consistently yet

These should be recorded: centrifugation selects flake size, and drying has been linked to long-term conductivity. We did not find a study isolating the effect of water washing alone.

10.1007/s41127-024-00076-8 · 10.1021/acs.chemmater.7b02847

5. Short sonication while mixing rule set · not recorded consistently yet

Worth recording because it costs little, but we make no claim that it changes a value: the evidence we found concerns sonication during delamination, not minutes of mixing.

10.1021/acs.chemmater.7b02847

6. Annealing a finished film in the map

Shown as post-treatment, since it can raise or lower conductivity depending on conditions. Whether a given value was measured after annealing still has to be read paper by paper.

10.1007/s41127-024-00076-8 · 10.1039/d5ra07980b

7. Recipe of another sample in the same paper in the map

Hidden, so it cannot be mistaken for how the sample behind a value was made. This is our display choice; the literature cannot settle it.

8. Unusually low conductivity for a «pure» film in the map

Kept and flagged rather than deleted. Good films are usually in the thousands of S/cm or higher, but low values do occur, so a low value alone does not prove an error.

10.1007/s41127-024-00076-8 · 10.1007/s40820-024-01398-1
